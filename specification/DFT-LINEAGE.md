## 1. Purpose

This document records the architectural provenance of IRP-1 without making the public protocol depend on acceptance of Digital Fabrica Theory (DFT) or related theoretical frameworks as scientific, physical, metaphysical, or external authority.

IRP-1 MUST remain independently understandable, implementable, falsifiable, testable, and verifiable from its public specification, code, test vectors, evidence bundles, consensus evidence, and verifier behavior.

## 2. Architectural provenance

IRP-1 draws design inspiration from a wider DFT-family architectural vocabulary, including:

- **DFT — Digital Fabrica Theory**;
- **OMT — Observer Monad Theory**;
- **TFR / Realica**;
- **PI-DST**.

The relevant contribution of this lineage is architectural rather than evidentiary.

## 3. Observer / action separation

A central lineage principle is to separate:

```text
what is observed or declared
from
what is transformed or acted upon
```

IRP-1 operationalizes that separation through the W0→W1→W2→W3 chain:

- W0 records the declared observation boundary;
- W1 records bounded declared interpretation and intended action;
- W2 records policy/approval/authorization state before execution;
- W3 records observed outcome after execution.

This is a protocol construction. It does not require a claim about the ontology of observers or reality.

## 4. Pre-action / post-action asymmetry

The protocol treats pre-action and post-action evidence as structurally different.

A post-hoc log can describe what a system later says happened, but it cannot by itself establish what was committed before execution.

IRP-1 therefore preserves a hard asymmetry:

```text
pre-action commitment
!=
post-action description
```

W2 is the decisive pre-execution authorization commitment. W3 records outcome without rewriting W2.

## 5. Traceability and invariant engineering

DFT-family work emphasizes traceability through transformations and invariants that survive process evolution.

IRP-1 extracts a narrow engineering form of that idea:

- deterministic canonicalization;
- domain-separated hashes;
- explicit parent linkage;
- stable session identity;
- replay controls;
- policy/version/input binding;
- explicit approval state;
- authorization/action-manifest binding;
- post-action correspondence evaluation;
- separated verifier dimensions.

These are conventional software/protocol constructs and MUST be justified by implementation and verification evidence, not by theoretical lineage.

## 6. Bounded transformation

IRP-1 models an agent operation as a bounded transition from declared pre-action state to observed post-action state under explicit policy and authority conditions.

That can be summarized as:

```text
Observation
-> Interpretation / Intent
-> Policy / Approval / Authorization
-> Execution
-> Outcome
-> Correspondence
```

The protocol does not claim that this chain captures the complete state of an AI system, environment, institution, or physical process.

## 7. Protocol extraction

The lineage contribution is the discipline of extracting a small, falsifiable, interoperable protocol from a broader conceptual system.

Accordingly, the public IRP-1 core MUST prefer:

- explicit schemas over metaphors;
- deterministic rules over interpretive prose;
- inspectable evidence over authority claims;
- bounded failure semantics over universal guarantees;
- interoperability over isolated terminology;
- implementation tests over theoretical prestige.

## 8. Independence rule

A third party MUST be able to implement IRP-1 without reading or accepting DFT, OMT, TFR, or PI-DST.

A verifier MUST be able to reject an invalid IRP-1 receipt without reference to those frameworks.

A valid IRP-1 receipt MUST NOT require any DFT-specific ontology, physics, cosmology, or metaphysical assumption.
